# Direct Controller startup and terminal scoring v2

New policy drafts use `direct_controller_v2`: after the physical owner is ready and the single Controller session is bound, the host starts the world and clock, then sends its first task prompt. Reading, observations and reasoning count toward the configured wall limit. A failed or uncertain first delivery is an engineering failure requiring review; it must not be reported as a task success or retried in the same world. Existing prepared runs keep their frozen inputs and original protocol.

Policy runs declare `terminal_outcome_v2` in the frozen run protocol. Placement tasks require the existing geometric, release, stability and assignment predicates; Dial uses its solved state. Relay requires all payloads to be released, supported and within the configured GOAL boundary tolerance (50 mm for the current released contract); earlier qualified handover, dropped flags and recorded violations do not independently veto terminal completion. Reports retain `strict_task_success`, process compliance and violations; Relay retains `legacy_process_goals_met`, `legacy_completed_payloads`, drops and handover records. A violation entry is deduplicated by kind and key, not a verified count of separate collision episodes. Failed actions and unresolved actions remain distinct from process violations. An interrupted or unresolved physical run is not promoted to success.

Dial success is latched once a simultaneous valid correct input unlocks the device; a later change to the dials does not undo it. This is an unlock event, rather than a requirement that the final dial positions remain correct.

`robot_contact_episodes` separately reports sampled contact episodes for inter-robot and self-robot body pairs using the existing collision thresholds (penetration greater than 0.2 mm and 1 mm respectively). Contact gaps of at most 50 ms of simulation time are joined. The report includes the maximum sampling gap and per-pair counts; it counts observed body-pair episodes, not unique physical impacts, and short missed contacts or multiple body pairs per impact can change the number. Legacy deduplicated violations and drop metrics are preserved; none of these counts changes terminal success.

Relay `drops` is the existing state-machine detection, not a complete count of every physical drop; it depends on established airborne, support and contact states. Transport, Mosaic and Dial do not provide a validated general drop counter in this evaluator. An absent metric means not provided, not zero events. Dial's red-operation support violations are detected only before unlock; the success latch is unaffected by later dial changes. Other events and their detection coverage are task-specific.

`public/inputs/policy-rules.md` is the common policy contract supplied to Codex and Claude; hosts retain different native tool transports. Its content and the actual model-facing inputs are frozen per run. The CLI model-plus-harness combination remains part of the experimental condition; equal task rules do not prove equal model context or tool behavior.

This changes both model-visible startup and scoring; compare results only after recording the protocol and scoring version. Historic results are not rewritten. Policy preparation applies versioned terminal-scoring public overlays to the reviewed legacy projections and applicable family guidance, and records hashes of the resulting files. The published source release remains unchanged.
