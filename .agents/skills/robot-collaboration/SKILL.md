# Robot collaboration

Use the frozen task and current role assignment. Read [tools](references/tools.md) before READY and [communication](references/communication.md) before coordinating commitments. At startup combine task goals with READY's static robot capabilities; distinguish configuration, timestamped evidence and assumptions. Load selected RGB images when needed; a path alone is not viewing.

Initial policy roles register READY before START, save waiting and end the startup turn. Runtime forks follow their current assignment rather than repeating team startup. After START choose to keep working, wait or save/end; one decision need not end a native turn. Meta runs infrastructure, not the task plan.

In adaptive mode read [bounded forks](references/adaptive.md) before READY; current runtime assignments identify delegated scope and connection.

## Rolling-horizon planning

Treat goals as final conditions and honor explicit task precedence; derive remaining order from dependencies and resources. List/slot indexing alone does not prescribe a schedule. Optimize completion time with supported concurrent work under the task's quality/risk objective and executor limits. Distinguish required precision from diagnostic thresholds; when the task accepts residual error, compare further correction with useful progress and its disturbance cost rather than treating each strict audit threshold as a mandatory stage gate.

Keep a compact frontier in the existing work note: active scopes, ready work, and blocked steps with their enabling fact. Plan successors while current scopes execute. At START, material milestones and chosen reviews, select the next concurrent set; refine only affected dependencies unless priorities or bottlenecks change. Authorize settled scopes while refining later work. Compare observed progress with expectations through relevant reports, execution state and chosen views; investigate unexplained inactivity rather than relying only on incoming messages. Choose review timing and evidence cost yourself.

Separate each scope's executable prefix from its first blocked operation. Bind prerequisites there: a later delivery can be blocked while source preparation or another job is ready. When a role would wait, examine its remaining prefixes and the next interactions of other roles. Compare ordering, stable buffer locations, paths or timing where they can unblock useful flow. Favor critical work and avoided downstream waiting over merely keeping a gripper occupied.

An exclusion covers a conflicting operation, swept space and time, with an observable release. A teammate moving or having an unfinished order is a reason to assess the relevant path, not a robot-wide mutex. Reuse valid clearance evidence within its coverage. If unknown overlap is the remaining reason to serialize, consider the optional motion screen described below. Retain the affected exclusion when evidence is insufficient while enabling other supported work.

Treat the current grasp, payload pose and full joint configuration as choices that shape the next interaction's feasible space. Before committing a costly or constrained segment, consider near successors: receiver approach, simultaneous support, release, withdrawal and free-gripper capacity. Compare alternatives when the current choice closes those options; refine the affected horizon as evidence changes, without exhaustively solving the episode before starting. Distinguish physical limits from your current reservation choices; negotiate changes before invalidating a commitment. Batch or stage extra items when saved downstream delay exceeds extra handling, occupied capacity and coordination cost. Count long holds, slip/recovery risk and blocked gripper capacity against the benefit of early preparation.

For dense placement or assembly, compare how the current order and pose affect later grasp access, placement, jaw opening, withdrawal and possible rework. An endpoint IK solution does not establish that complete manipulation segment. Where fingers cannot descend alongside neighboring objects, consider grasp height, approach orientation, placement order or release near the support surface. Account for the object's remaining fall and the fingers' opening/withdrawal space; choose release height from geometry and evidence rather than a fixed drop distance. Later contact can disturb completed subgoals: reverify the affected results when relevant evidence changes, rather than treating progress as permanent. For correction compare regrasping, controlled single/group pushes, temporary removal or another allocation by contact geometry, observability and total rework cost. Pushes use the existing finite moves, not a guaranteed alignment or force-control primitive; inspect the effects on neighboring objects.

## Role responsibility

Use the current mode's lifecycle. Single (centralized) has one Controller that plans and operates all robots, including concurrent actions. Hierarchical has a Coordinator and standing Workers; peer has standing Workers coordinating directly. These Workers remain team members after local completion. Adaptive has a Controller and optional temporary delegates: execute a bounded assignment and hand it back through return_task; use the adaptive reference for that lifecycle. An ended native reply alone ends neither team membership nor a delegation.

At START each Worker has standing authority to inspect, plan and perform supported independent local execution/preparation for the team task with its bound robot. Local means no unresolved joint commitment or shared-resource use, not everything inside its TCP bounds. Coordinate those commitments through the role's permitted route; accepted restrictions remain binding. In hierarchical mode Coordinator manages joint allocation, priorities and reservations. Workers choose local poses, speeds, views and commands, and propose useful scope changes while blocked. Authorize supported continuations early; an observable release within scope can enable execution without another approval.

Peers own their robots' plans and negotiate only affected commitments. Start uncontested local work while negotiating transfers; a request alone is not acceptance. Avoid whole-team approval barriers. All modes share physical tools and evidence limits.

## Execution and evidence

Use agent-authored finite action chunks when the next calls are already decided. In Desktop, robotSequence can review ordinary mail at the end of a self-contained segment with stable commitments. In the CLI, submit chosen calls through the bound robot tool, reviewing returned events at each boundary. Mail is retained; failures, unresolved execution, system events, pause/end and intermediate images return control. New evidence needing interpretation or an unresolved shared condition remains a model boundary. Use nonblocking calls or concurrent submissions when useful work can overlap.

Use READY’s actual capabilities and limits when choosing pose, aperture, speeds or joint targets. Initial supply location does not assign color ownership; shared goals remain shared unless the task specifies otherwise. For a reach or clearance problem, consider another supported pose, approach or teammate. Account for fingers and payload through placement, opening and retreat; command bounds or endpoint IK alone do not establish a usable path.

For a shared payload, plan the relative motion of all supporting tools as well as their individual paths: same-cycle start does not guarantee matched arrival or prevent internal loads. Preserve receiver access and sender withdrawal when selecting a grasp. Establish support from allowed visual/robot evidence before releasing an existing hold; width-based grasp feedback alone is insufficient. Choose direct concurrent control or a bounded multi-robot delegate by coupling and feedback cost, rather than splitting coupled support across independent scopes by habit.

Choose aperture motion or a force-held grasp by intent and available capabilities. A grasp's expected width and tolerances describe the object estimate; a settled width or drive-reported is_grasped is not proof of payload support. Maintain/release the selected grasp deliberately, and verify retention when relevant evidence changes. Ending force control does not establish release. Before withdrawing to leave a payload behind, assess measured aperture and allowed visual evidence that the jaws have cleared it; an opening failure or a payload following the tool calls for reassessing the release. Tool semantics are in the tool reference.

For a movable mechanism, distinguish its own reference frame from the world frame. Choose the support contact and bearing direction for load moments, not jaw aperture alone; if a lift changes payload pose unexpectedly, reassess that geometry before repeating the same motion. Support pose and grasp must resist the partner's reaction forces while preserving approach, articulation and withdrawal options; update the shared reference after slip or reorientation. Treat support and manipulation as coupled work when choosing action or delegation boundaries.

Sustained contact or pose holding can be useful task execution after a motion ends. Choose its release from the task and current commitments; a tool return, waiting turn or control handback does not itself require releasing. When interpreting apparatus feedback, bind it to a timestamped observed input configuration, separating intended settings, estimates and evidence. A contradiction can reflect either reasoning assumptions or execution/observation error.

Track the robot with proprioception and objects with RGB: a commanded TCP position is not a measured object position. For precision placement, relate the task's object anchor/pose to the observed object-to-tool offset instead of equating their centers; transport can change that offset through slip. Choose sufficiently informative views or a supported observation pose when occlusion dominates; repeated computation on the same ambiguous view adds no physical evidence. Grasp offsets and release/retreat can displace an object. Verify grasp after lifting and placement after clearing; consider vertical clearance before lateral retreat where geometry permits. Report observed object state separately from command targets and remaining uncertainty. Release and path clearance are distinct; report the earliest verified release that changes another role's options. Current tools do not certify estimated mid-motion clearance, and TCP separation alone is insufficient.

When unclear full-arm overlap would cause a wait or cancellation, consider optional motion screening to compare candidates; its interface is in the tool reference. Reuse reports/screening within their coverage, and assess whether changes affect relevant paths. Reobserve for stale, conflicting or insufficient evidence. After transport uncertainty query/retry the original ID rather than replay physical work under a new ID.

## Exploration and run-local experience

Choose trials and adjustments from the task's permitted observations and robot feedback. Exploration is part of task execution: its time, actions and failures count, including trials before the first successful placement. A separate learning phase exists only when declared by the experiment; there is no compulsory pre-task probing sequence. Engineering oracle checks are not policy experience or a source of state-derived recipes.

Keep useful lessons with the frontier in your work note: attempted method, observed outcome/evidence, applicable robot/object/pose conditions and uncertainty. Distinguish commands and estimates from measured facts. Revise lessons when new evidence contradicts them; one success is not universal calibration. Lessons inform choices within the frozen rules and current commitments.

If substantial experience outgrows the compact note, write an optional self-authored `public/results/<actor>-experience.md` in this run and retain its reference in work. This is derived guidance, not a modification of the frozen Skill. Share selected lessons through the permitted communication route; receivers check applicability. Cross-run reuse is off by default; an explicitly authorized learning protocol must declare the retained sources and learning cost.

## Active waiting and context

Before directing a role or yourself to wait, review remaining work for an independently executable portion. Identify the blocked step, actual resource/missing evidence and next review milestone. Propose a specific scope change through the permitted route; explicit restrictions remain binding until changed.

For Single/Adaptive Controllers, Coordinators and standing Workers, choose continued work, wait_events within the active turn, or a saved wait before ending a reply. Standing Workers remain available until team end. Adaptive delegates normally continue their bounded work through necessary tool waits and return_task when handing it back; ending a reply with a saved review is an explicit alternative, not a required phase. Whenever a role chooses yield waiting/available during an active run, supply a finite self-selected after_s based on expected progress and review cost. On review merge events, continue enabled work or propose a useful adjustment/request evidence. Reassess waiting rather than merely repeat that authorization is missing. Update pending proposals when facts or delay materially change the decision; elapsed time alone needs no chat message. Normal waiting is not failure.

Keep the frontier, commitments and verified progress in one compact note. Update satisfied prerequisites explicitly; saved blockers can become stale while another scope executes. Save material changes and before ending. Restore work/history after context gaps; newer mail/executor facts supersede stale notes. Consider late mail returned with yield. The role authorized to finish verifies the whole task; other roles verify their assigned scope before returning. Physical-owner loss ends the run; Meta handles engineering recovery.
